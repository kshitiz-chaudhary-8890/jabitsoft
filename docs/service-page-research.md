# Single Service Page Research

Last reviewed: 17 September 2026

## Goal

Define one reusable, conversion-focused page architecture for JabitSoft's six service pages:

- Agentic AI Development
- Cloud Consulting
- Mobile Application Development
- ERP Services
- SEO / Digital Marketing
- Website Solutions

The shared structure should make each service understandable, credible, and easy to act on while allowing the content and supporting visual to change by service.

## Research Method

This review uses first-party service pages from established B2B technology and consulting providers. It focuses on the information architecture and trust mechanisms they use, not on copying their visual style or claims.

Primary references:

- [IBM AI Consulting](https://www.ibm.com/consulting/artificial-intelligence)

## What the Strongest Pages Have in Common

### 1. They lead with an outcome, not a technology list

The opening explains the business change the buyer can expect, then identifies the service. Accenture frames cloud as an engine for AI, innovation, and growth; AWS opens with business transformation and faster value; Netguru's mobile page promises end-to-end ownership from architecture through launch and iteration.

Recommendation: the JabitSoft hero should contain one outcome-led headline, a short positioning paragraph, one primary CTA, one lower-commitment secondary CTA, and a service-specific visual. Do not put a long capability list in the hero.

Sources: [Accenture Cloud Consulting](https://www.accenture.com/en/services/cloud), [AWS Professional Services](https://aws.amazon.com/professional-services/), [Netguru Mobile App Development](https://www.netguru.com/services/mobile-development)

### 2. They establish proof near the top

High-performing pages quickly show evidence: client logos, delivery statistics, quantified outcomes, recognitions, or a short customer result. Netguru places scale metrics and client logos immediately after its web-app hero. Thoughtworks follows its CX proposition with quantified commercial results. WebFX puts growth, satisfaction, ratings, and its measurement approach near the opening.

Recommendation: add a compact proof strip immediately after the hero. Use only verifiable JabitSoft facts. If strong numbers are unavailable, use named clients, years of experience, supported platforms, certifications, or one short outcome statement rather than invented metrics.

Sources: [Netguru Web Application Development](https://www.netguru.com/services/web-application-development-services), [Thoughtworks Customer Experience](https://www.thoughtworks.com/what-we-do/customer-experience), [WebFX SEO Services](https://www.webfx.com/seo/services/)

### 3. They explain the buyer's problem before detailing the offer

Good pages establish why the service matters now and identify the friction that prevents results. IBM's AI page explains the gap between AI experimentation and scaling value. EPAM's enterprise systems page starts with the complexity of core processes and systems. This makes the subsequent capability list feel like a response rather than a catalogue.

Recommendation: use a concise "The challenge" section with three concrete business problems and a short bridge to the proposed outcome. It should be written for decision-makers, not as a generic technology definition.

Sources: [IBM AI Consulting](https://www.ibm.com/consulting/artificial-intelligence), [EPAM Core & Enterprise Systems](https://www.epam.com/services/engineering/core-and-enterprise-systems)

### 4. They separate capabilities from use cases

IBM distinguishes AI capabilities such as strategy, data, and agentic AI from departmental use cases such as supply chain, marketing, security, and finance. Netguru separates mobile service scope from platform choices and separates web-app delivery services from the types of applications it builds. That distinction helps buyers answer both "what can you do?" and "is this relevant to me?"

Recommendation: keep two separate sections:

- Capabilities: the concrete work JabitSoft performs.
- Use cases / solutions: the business situations those capabilities solve.

Sources: [IBM AI Consulting](https://www.ibm.com/consulting/artificial-intelligence), [Netguru Mobile App Development](https://www.netguru.com/services/mobile-development), [Netguru Web Application Development](https://www.netguru.com/services/web-application-development-services)

### 5. They show how delivery works

Process reduces perceived project risk. Netguru presents a discovery-to-launch mobile lifecycle. EPAM describes an Explore, Discover, Design, Deliver, Run & Scale model. EPAM also frames enterprise-system work across advisory, implementation, engineering, and operations.

Recommendation: show a five-step delivery model that stays consistent across all services:

1. Discover
2. Define
3. Design
4. Build and validate
5. Launch, measure, and improve

The labels can stay consistent while each service changes the deliverables beneath them.

Sources: [Netguru Mobile App Development](https://www.netguru.com/services/mobile-development), [EPAM Core & Enterprise Systems](https://www.epam.com/services/engineering/core-and-enterprise-systems)

### 6. They prove the work with a specific story

IBM's AI and SAP pages include named case studies and outcomes. AWS shows customer examples across migration, AI, and modernization. Netguru's web-app page explains client expectations, technology, delivery setup, and the result. Specific before-and-after evidence is more persuasive than a generic testimonial.

Recommendation: every service page should include one featured case study with:

- Client or anonymized industry context
- Starting problem
- JabitSoft intervention
- Measurable or observable result
- Relevant technologies
- Link to the full case study when available

Sources: [IBM AI Consulting](https://www.ibm.com/consulting/artificial-intelligence), [IBM SAP Consulting](https://www.ibm.com/consulting/sap), [AWS Professional Services](https://aws.amazon.com/professional-services/), [Netguru Web Application Development](https://www.netguru.com/services/web-application-development-services)

### 7. They address delivery risk, not just features

The strongest pages discuss security, governance, integration, ownership, support, total cost, time-to-value, or ongoing operations. AWS makes scale and security a core offering. IBM emphasizes responsible AI, governance, and trusted deployment. Netguru answers pricing, timelines, intellectual-property ownership, engagement models, and post-launch support in its FAQ.

Recommendation: add a service-specific "Built for production" section and an FAQ. These should answer the objections most likely to stop a buyer from contacting JabitSoft.

Sources: [AWS Professional Services](https://aws.amazon.com/professional-services/), [IBM AI Consulting](https://www.ibm.com/consulting/artificial-intelligence), [Netguru Web Application Development](https://www.netguru.com/services/web-application-development-services)

### 8. They close with one clear next step

All reviewed pages repeat a direct contact or project-estimation action after the buyer has seen scope and proof. The call to action is stronger when it describes the next step rather than using a vague label.

Recommendation: use one final CTA such as "Discuss your project" with expectation-setting copy: what the visitor should bring, who they will speak with, and what they will receive from the first conversation.

Sources: [AWS Professional Services](https://aws.amazon.com/professional-services/), [EPAM Engineering](https://www.epam.com/services/engineering), [Netguru Mobile App Development](https://www.netguru.com/services/mobile-development)

## Recommended Reusable Information Architecture

### 1. Service Hero — Essential

Content:

- Service eyebrow
- Outcome-led H1
- 2–3 line positioning statement
- Primary CTA: `Discuss your project`
- Secondary CTA: `See our capabilities` or `View relevant work`
- One service-specific visual or interactive system

Design note: keep the hero distinct from the generic Services landing page. Its visual should communicate the service concept, not decorate it.

### 2. Proof Strip — Essential

Content options:

- 3 verified metrics
- 4–6 relevant client logos
- Certifications or partnerships
- One quantified client outcome plus two credibility facts

Design note: use a clean horizontal rhythm rather than separate floating cards.

### 3. The Business Challenge — Essential

Content:

- Short context statement
- Three buyer pain points
- One transition into JabitSoft's response

Purpose: demonstrate understanding of the buyer before describing the solution.

### 4. Capabilities — Essential

Content:

- 4–6 specific service offerings
- Short outcome attached to each capability
- Optional link to a deeper sub-service when content exists

Design note: capability blocks should not all look like identical generic cards. Use an editorial list, numbered grid, or interactive details panel.

### 5. Use Cases / Solutions — Essential

Content:

- 4–6 situations the service solves
- Audience, department, or industry context
- Expected operational or customer outcome

Purpose: let buyers quickly recognise themselves on the page.

### 6. Delivery Process — Essential

Use the shared five-stage model:

1. Discover
2. Define
3. Design
4. Build and validate
5. Launch, measure, and improve

Each service supplies its own deliverables, decision gates, and success measures.

### 7. Featured Case Study — Essential

Content:

- Problem
- Intervention
- Outcome
- Technology / platform
- Full case-study link

Fallback: if a publishable service-specific project does not exist, use a clearly labeled representative engagement without fabricated client identity or results.

### 8. Technology and Ecosystem — Essential, but secondary

Content:

- Relevant platforms, frameworks, and integrations
- Why the stack or partner ecosystem matters
- Security, accessibility, performance, or compliance considerations

Design note: avoid an unexplained logo wall. Group technologies by role and connect them to business value.

### 9. Why JabitSoft — Essential

Use 3–4 defensible differentiators, for example:

- Business-first discovery
- Design and engineering working as one team
- Integration with existing systems
- Support beyond launch

Avoid unsupported superlatives and generic claims such as "best-in-class."

### 10. FAQ / Buying Questions — Essential

Minimum questions:

- What does the engagement include?
- How long does a typical project take?
- How is scope and cost estimated?
- Can JabitSoft work with our existing team and systems?
- Who owns the deliverables and source code?
- What happens after launch?

Add service-specific questions for security, compliance, migration, platform selection, SEO timelines, or AI governance.

### 11. Final CTA — Essential

Content:

- Short outcome-led heading
- One sentence explaining the first step
- One primary action
- Optional contact reassurance, such as response time, only if operationally accurate

### 12. Related Services / Insights — Optional

Use only when it helps the buyer continue a relevant journey. Keep it below the final proof and above or integrated with the shared footer. Do not dilute the primary CTA with a large content feed.

## Optional Sections and When to Use Them

### Industry Tabs

Use when the service changes substantially across industries and JabitSoft has genuine examples or domain expertise. IBM's AI page uses departmental use cases; EPAM's enterprise work demonstrates industry-specific solutions.

### Pricing or Engagement Models

Use for SEO and standardized discovery packages where realistic ranges can improve lead quality. Netguru's web-app page shows price and engagement-model guidance, while WebFX exposes plan starting points. For complex ERP, AI, or cloud programmes, explain the estimation method rather than publish false precision.

Sources: [Netguru Web Application Development](https://www.netguru.com/services/web-application-development-services), [WebFX SEO Services](https://www.webfx.com/seo/services/)

### Experts / Team

Use on AI, Cloud, or ERP pages when named specialists, credentials, or leadership materially increase confidence. IBM explicitly introduces experts on its AI page. Do not add anonymous stock-profile cards.

Source: [IBM AI Consulting](https://www.ibm.com/consulting/artificial-intelligence)

### Partnerships and Certifications

Use when they directly support delivery credibility, such as cloud partnerships, ERP platform experience, or certifications. IBM and AWS make ecosystems and platform expertise part of their proof.

Sources: [IBM AI Consulting](https://www.ibm.com/consulting/artificial-intelligence), [AWS Professional Services](https://aws.amazon.com/professional-services/)

### Insights

Use a maximum of three tightly relevant articles when the buying cycle benefits from education, especially Agentic AI, Cloud, ERP, and SEO. Keep this secondary to proof and conversion.

## Sections to Avoid

- A hero followed immediately by a wall of generic cards.
- Repeating the same capability content under different headings.
- Technology-logo walls without context.
- Unverified metrics, client claims, certifications, or testimonials.
- Long "What is this service?" textbook copy before showing relevance.
- Multiple competing CTAs in every section.
- FAQ content written only for keyword coverage rather than buyer concerns.
- Using the same visual metaphor for all six services.

## Recommended Final Flow

1. Outcome-led service hero
2. Proof strip
3. Business challenge
4. Capabilities
5. Use cases / solutions
6. Delivery process
7. Featured case study
8. Technology, ecosystem, and production safeguards
9. Why JabitSoft
10. FAQ / buying questions
11. Final CTA
12. Optional related services or insights

## Component Architecture Recommendation

Build one data-driven `ServiceDetailPage` shell with reusable structural components, while allowing a service to replace the hero visual, case-study treatment, and one signature section.

Shared components:

- `ServiceHero`
- `ServiceProofStrip`
- `ServiceChallenge`
- `ServiceCapabilities`
- `ServiceUseCases`
- `ServiceProcess`
- `ServiceCaseStudy`
- `ServiceTechnology`
- `ServiceReasons`
- `ServiceFaq`
- `ServiceCta`

Per-service content should live in that service's folder as structured data. Each service folder may also contain:

- One custom hero visual
- One optional signature section
- Service-specific assets

This keeps the six pages consistent without making them visually identical or reducing the work to a simple title-and-description swap.

## Implementation Priority

1. Remove the current experimental single-service components only after confirming the shared route remains functional.
2. Build the shared shell and content schema.
3. Complete Agentic AI as the reference implementation with real content and proof.
4. Validate desktop, tablet, mobile, keyboard use, reduced motion, and loading performance.
5. Reuse the approved system for the remaining five services, changing content, visual, use cases, risks, and proof.
